SPACE MISSION DESIGNER — ENGINEERING MODEL & GAME BALANCE

SCOPE
This is an educational decision simulator. It models the relationships between equipment selection, mass, launch capacity, power, communications, mission events, science return and survival resources. It does not predict real flight trajectories or replace aerospace analysis tools.

CORE FORMULAS
Payload mass = sum of selected non-launch-vehicle equipment masses.
Payload utilization (%) = payload mass / simulated vehicle capacity * 100.
Power margin (kW) = sum of selected generation - sum of selected draw.
Mission validation requires a selected launch vehicle, spacecraft, power source, communications system and instrument; mass must not exceed simulated capacity; power margin must meet the mission's minimum; destination-specific rules must be satisfied.

Mission power margins in the prototype:
Earth: 0.5 kW minimum
Moon: 0.5 kW minimum
Mars: 1.0 kW minimum

MISSION LOOP
Earth: orbital observation decisions (cloud cover, orbital drift, data downlink), data collection/transmission and mission report.
Moon: transfer decisions, powered descent sequence, EVA, 3 geology samples, terrain scan, beacon deployment, rover driving, safe return.
Mars: deep-space transfer decisions, simplified entry/descent sequence, EVA, 3 rock cores, regolith scan, weather/navigation beacon, rover driving, safe return.

FLIGHT DECISIONS
Each mission event changes some combination of power reserve, communications link, vehicle integrity, science data, score and elapsed mission days. The values are game-state deltas, not real spacecraft telemetry.

SURFACE RESOURCES
Oxygen reserve and suit battery decrease during EVA. Rover battery decreases while driving and boosting. The player must finish objectives and return to the lander before resources reach unsafe thresholds. Surface temperature is displayed as a mission setting, not a thermodynamic model.

SCIENCE DATA
Sample collection, terrain analysis and communications increase the science return. The mission report records collected samples, scan status, beacon status, return confirmation, data and final resource levels.

KNOWN SIMPLIFICATIONS
- Mars atmospheric entry, heat shield and parachute dynamics are not physically solved; the descent is a timed gameplay sequence.
- Lunar/Martian gravity, walking and rover motion are simplified gameplay controls.
- Rocket ascent and booster separation are staged visual animation, not a validated orbital mechanics solver.
- Power and communications are resource meters, not detailed electrical or RF simulations.
- Prices, capacities, durations, mass and power values are prototype balancing values.
