SPACE MISSION DESIGNER — LATEST GAMEPLAY BUILD
Unity Editor: 6000.6.8f1
Main scene: Assets/Project_/Scenes/MainMenu.unity

WHAT CHANGED

1) EARTH OBSERVATION
- Once the orbital flight events are completed, the Earth mission now opens an orbital imaging operations screen instead of jumping directly to Results.
- The left panel renders a 3D Earth globe with the project's Earth_Diffuse map, atmospheric shell, an imaging satellite and its solar wings.
- Capture buttons lock the orbital camera onto the selected region and show a target reticle: Amazonas Basin, North Atlantic, or Central Sahara.
- The capture action requires the Multispectral Imager (shown internally as SCIENCE CAMERA) and costs 6 gameplay power units per frame.
- Captured frames must be downlinked over the X-band communication system. Each downlink batch costs 3 gameplay power units.
- At least two frames must be captured and transmitted before the survey can be completed.
- This is a local 3D mission simulation, not a live satellite feed or live NASA imagery service.

2) MOON AND MARS SURFACE OPERATIONS
- Mars and Moon now use visibly different suit palettes, terrain colors and terrain relief.
- The Mars suit uses a tan/rust pressure-suit palette; the lunar EVA suit remains white/gray with darker blue/gold components.
- Movement uses approximate surface-gravity values: Moon 1.62 m/s^2 and Mars 3.71 m/s^2. Jump arcs and movement speeds differ accordingly.
- A visible, illuminated EVA perimeter marks the authorized area. The astronaut is blocked from crossing it and receives a TURN BACK warning near the limit. The rover is clamped to the same operational zone.
- Right mouse or middle mouse drag rotates the view. Mouse wheel zooms. WASD/arrow keys move, Shift sprints, Space jumps, Q enters/exits the rover, E collects a sample, F runs a terrain/regolith analysis, B deploys the beacon, and R returns to the lander when the objectives are complete and the astronaut is close enough.

3) LAUNCH PAD
- The Earth-side launch setting includes distant mission-control buildings, control-room windows, rooftop antenna dishes, an observation tower and service/access roads. These belong to the surface environment and disappear during the transition to space.

4) EQUIPMENT AND COSTS
- Equipment cards use clearer engineering descriptions such as medium-lift/heavy-lift launch vehicle, Earth-observation spacecraft bus, powered descent lander, deployable photovoltaic array, long-life power module, X-band high-gain antenna and multispectral imager.
- Values are mission-planning credits (CR) balanced for gameplay. They are not actual vendor quotes, NASA procurement prices, or certified vehicle performance specifications.

OPENING THE PROJECT
1. Extract this ZIP to a new folder.
2. In Unity Hub choose Add / Open and select the extracted project root — the folder containing Assets, Packages and ProjectSettings.
3. Open Assets/Project_/Scenes/MainMenu.unity and press Play.

IMPORTANT TESTING NOTE
The Unity Editor is not installed in the file-generation environment, so this build was checked for project structure, ZIP integrity, and basic C# delimiter/string balance, but it was not compiled or Play-tested in Unity. If Unity reports a compiler error, use the Console's first red error as the starting point.

VISUALS NOTE
The current launch pad, spacecraft, astronaut, rover, satellite and surface environment are procedural Unity models. This update improves composition and gameplay presentation, but does not add photorealistic third-party FBX assets.
